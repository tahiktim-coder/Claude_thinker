# Hidden Monopolies: Small-Cap Companies With Near-Monopoly Positions

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report identifies 11 small-cap companies (under $3B market cap) that hold near-monopoly or dominant positions in critical niches. These are not household names -- they are the sole suppliers, only FDA-approved options, or patent-protected leaders in markets most investors have never heard of.

**Key finding:** The strongest monopolies combine regulatory protection + high switching costs + growing end markets. The weakest are merely "market leaders" in competitive spaces dressed up as monopolies.

### #1. CEVA, Inc. (NASDAQ: CEVA)
**The Invisible Monopoly Inside Your Phone**

| Gross Margin | 88.1% |

**THE MONOPOLY:** CEVA licenses semiconductor IP -- the wireless connectivity and sensor processing blueprints that chipmakers embed into their chips. CEVA holds ~70% market share in wireless connectivity IP (Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, 5G baseband). Their IP has shipped in over 20 BILLION devices across 400+ customers. When any chipmaker wants to add Bluetooth or Wi-Fi to a chip, there's a ~70% chance they're licensing it from CEVA.

**HOW DOMINANT:** 70% market share in wireless IP. Every device that ships with their IP pays royalties for the life of the product. This is a toll-booth business model with 88% gross margins.

**MOAT DURATION:** 10-15 years. IP licensing moats compound -- once designed into a chip, switching costs are astronomical (redesign the entire silicon). The installed base of 20B+ devices creates network effects in developer tools and ecosystem.

**WHAT BREAKS IT:** Arm expanding into NPUs (edge AI), China revenue concentration (49%), or a major customer insourcing. Apple's potential modem insourcing is actually a TAILWIND -- UBS estimates $10M+ additional royalty revenue in 2027 if Apple transitions 70% of its 2026 lineup to internal modems using CEVA IP.

**INSIDER SIGNAL: BULLISH.** CEO Amir Panush purchased 5,100 shares ($100K). CFO Yaniv Arieli bought 2,500 shares ($48K). Director Jaclyn Liu bought 1,310 shares ($25K). THREE insiders buying, ZERO selling in the past 6 months.

**10x MATH:** At 10x, market cap = $5.07B. Would need ~$500M revenue at current multiples. With 20B+ installed devices generating royalties and edge AI licensing ramping (10 NeuPro NPU agreements signed in 2025, AI now 20%+ of licensing revenue), this is achievable by 2030-2032 if AI-at-the-edge adoption accelerates.

**CONVICTION: 9/10** -- Real monopoly, proven by 88% gross margins and 20B device installs. Insider buying confirms.

**The ONLY Drug That Prevents Chemo-Induced Deafness in Children**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Price | $7.83 |
| Market Cap | $268M |

**THE MONOPOLY:** Pedmark is the FIRST AND ONLY FDA-approved therapy to reduce the risk of hearing loss (ototoxicity) from cisplatin chemotherapy in pediatric cancer patients aged 1 month and older. There is literally no alternative. If a child is getting cisplatin chemo and you want to protect their hearing, Pedmark is the only option.

**HOW DOMINANT:** 100% market share -- there is no competitor. Pedmark is the standard-of-care-in-waiting for cisplatin-treated pediatric patients.

**MOAT DURATION:** 5-8 years minimum. FDA approval + orphan-drug-like dynamics. Any competitor would need to run multi-year clinical trials and gain FDA approval. The market is too small for big pharma to bother competing.

**WHAT BREAKS IT:** Single-asset risk (Pedmark is the ONLY product). Pricing pressure. Slow adoption. If cisplatin usage declines in favor of alternative chemotherapy agents, the addressable market shrinks.

**INSIDER SIGNAL: MIXED.** CFO Robert Andrade bought $334K in shares (bullish). But Southpoint Capital (10% owner) sold $2M in late 2025, and Director Rosty Raykov sold $75K (both on pre-arranged plans).

Global expansion (Japan Phase 2/3 data positive, entering international markets) could push toward $150-200M by 2028-2029. 10x is ambitious but not impossible if Pedmark becomes standard-of-care globally. A 5x ($1.3B) is more realistic.

**CONVICTION: 8.5/10** -- True 100% monopoly with no competitor, but single-asset concentration risk is real.

The capital intensity of ion implant for SiC is 5x that of traditional silicon fabs, and Axcelis's Purion suite of three specialized tools vastly outperforms Applied Materials' single tool.

**10x MATH:** At current $2.4B, 10x = $24B. Ion implant equipment demand projected to reach $6B by 2030 (1,200+ tools). If Axcelis/Veeco captures 40% of that, that's $2.4B in annual revenue at 20x P/S = $48B. But the merger complicates pure 10x math. A 3-4x ($7-10B) over 5 years is more realistic.

**CONVICTION: 8/10** -- Real 70-80% monopoly in a growing niche, but cyclical trough + merger uncertainty.

Path to 10x exists but depends entirely on gene therapy adoption. Management targets $200M revenue run rate at 70%+ gross margins -- if achieved, 10x is very real.

**CONVICTION: 8/10** -- One of the strongest regulatory moats I've found. The risk is execution and cash burn, not competition.
