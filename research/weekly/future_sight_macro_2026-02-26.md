# PART 1: WHERE IS THE WORLD ACTUALLY HEADING?

## 1. AI AGENTS REPLACING HUMAN WORKFLOWS

**The Verdict: This is the most important technology trend since the internet itself.**

The numbers tell the story:
- Agentic AI market: $8.5B in 2026 -> $45B by 2030 -> Google projects $1T by 2040
- 57% of companies already have AI agents in production (G2 survey, Aug 2025)
- Gartner: 40% of enterprise applications will include task-specific AI agents by end of 2026
- 89% of CIOs consider agent-based AI a strategic priority
- Anthropic now captures 40% of enterprise LLM spend (up from 12% two years ago)

**What the agentic stack looks like:**
- **Foundation models** (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) -- the brains
- **Orchestration layer** (UiPath Maestro, Salesforce Agentforce, ServiceNow) -- the coordinator
- **Memory/Context infrastructure** (vector databases, retrieval systems) -- the memory
- **Action layer** (API connectors, browser automation, code execution) -- the hands
- **Observability/Safety** (guardrails, monitoring, evaluation) -- the oversight

**Key insight:** The "agent orchestration" layer is where the money is. Whoever becomes the "Windows of AI agents" -- the layer that manages, deploys, and coordinates multiple agents -- wins an enormous market. Salesforce's Agentforce already hit $1.8B ARR. Microsoft Power Automate is deeply integrated. UiPath is pivoting hard but is late.

**The real winners are not who you expect.** The hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Salesforce) are building agent platforms themselves. The small company opportunity is in the *picks and shovels*: training data (INOD), evaluation/safety (emerging), and specialized vertical agents nobody else is building.

## 2. AI REPLACING DRUG DISCOVERY

**The Verdict: The disruption is real, but the timeline is longer than bulls think, and a new apex predator has appeared.**

**Isomorphic Labs just changed the game.** In February 2026, DeepMind's drug discovery spinoff unveiled IsoDDE (Drug Design Engine), which:
- Doubles AlphaFold 3's accuracy on difficult protein-ligand systems
- 2.3x improvement over AlphaFold 3 on antibody-antigen structures
- Can discover novel cryptic pockets from sequence alone
- Has $3B in partnerships with Eli Lilly, Novartis, and now J&J
- Expects first AI-designed drugs in clinical trials by end of 2026

This is the elephant in the room for both RXRX and SDGR. Isomorphic Labs has:
- Unlimited compute (Google)
- The world's best structural biology AI team (DeepMind)
- $3B+ in big pharma partnerships
- A proprietary model they refuse to open-source

**What this means for RXRX and SDGR:** They are not dead -- wet lab validation and phenomic data still matter -- but their competitive moats just got dramatically narrower. The physics-based and phenomics-based approaches need to prove they find things that IsoDDE cannot. So far, neither has a commercially approved drug.

**Key supply chain opportunities:**

- Actuators/motors: Mainly Asian suppliers (Harmonic Drive, etc.)
- AI brains: NVIDIA, Qualcomm

**Timeline assessment:** 2025-2026 = prototype/pilot (thousands of units). 2027-2028 = early production (tens of thousands). 2029-2030 = mass market (hundreds of thousands to millions). The component suppliers benefit BEFORE the robots ship at scale, because every prototype needs chips.

## 5. EDGE AI EVERYWHERE

## 6. QUANTUM COMPUTING

**The Verdict: Real science, but 5-10 years from commercial impact. Overhyped for 2026-2028.**

- Market: ~$1B revenue in 2025. Projected $170B by 2040.
- Useful commercial applications: 5-10 years away for most use cases
- IonQ, Rigetti, D-Wave all burning cash rapidly
- IBM Nighthawk (360 qubits) delivering in 2026 -- still far from "useful" scale
- Pure-play quantum stocks have valuations that assume everything goes right

**My take:** Quantum will eventually be transformative for drug discovery, materials science, cryptography, and optimization. But at current valuations, the pure plays (IONQ, RGTI, QBTS, QUBT) are lottery tickets, not investments. The smart play is owning companies that will *use* quantum (pharma, materials) rather than companies trying to *build* quantum computers.
