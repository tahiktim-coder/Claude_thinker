## The Thesis: Why AI Changes Drug Discovery

Traditional drug development takes 10-15 years and costs $1B+ per drug. AI compresses
both axes:

- **Speed:** AI-discovered drugs move from target to clinic in 3-6 years (40% faster).
- **Success rates:** Phase I success jumps from 40-65% (traditional) to 80-90% (AI). Phase II from 30-45% to 65-75%. This is the real edge -- most drug failures happen in Phase II.
- **Cost:** 30-70% reduction in preclinical costs.
- **Scale:** Recursion screens billions of compounds; Schrodinger simulates molecular physics at scale. This is not incremental improvement -- it is a structural shift in how drugs are found.

The market is $1.94B today, projected $16.49B by 2034 (27% CAGR). Goldman estimates AI
drug discovery could add 0.5-2.5% to US GDP. McKinsey pegs pharma efficiency gains at
$110B annually.

**Key milestone:** Insilico Medicine's rentosertib (ISM001-055) posted the first-ever
positive Phase IIa results for a fully AI-discovered drug in idiopathic pulmonary
fibrosis -- 98.4 mL FVC improvement vs -62.3 mL for placebo. This is proof of concept
that the thesis works. But no AI-discovered drug has received FDA approval yet. The first
is projected for 2026-2027 (60% probability). FDA final guidance on AI in drug
development is expected Q2 2026.

There are now 200+ AI-designed drugs in clinical development and 15-20 entering pivotal
trials in 2026. The pipeline is no longer hypothetical.

**Bottom line:** The thesis is real. The question is pricing.

## The RXRX Consensus Problem

This section exists because RXRX is the stock every "AI drug discovery" listicle
mentions first. That is exactly the problem.

**Why it is consensus:**
- Every article about AI drug discovery leads with Recursion. It is the default name.
- Cathie Wood's ARK Invest holds it. Retail loves the AI narrative.
- JPMorgan upgraded it. Seeking Alpha analysts write about it constantly.
- It is the largest pure-play by market cap, so index funds and ETFs must hold it.
- Ask any chatbot "best AI biotech stock" and RXRX comes first. If the answer is that easy to find, it is priced in.

**Why consensus is dangerous here:**
- The stock is at $4.06. It was at $12+ within the past year. Down ~67% from 52-week highs. Being consensus did not protect investors from this drawdown.
- No products on the market. No Phase 3 programs. Revenue is from partnership milestones ($100M+ expected by end-2026), not product sales.
- Burning cash at -$0.30/share per quarter. Dilution risk is real and ongoing.
- The "AI drug discovery" label attracts narrative-driven retail capital that can evaporate quickly on any bad news.
- Bank of America maintained Hold in January 2026. Not everyone is bullish.

**The bull case is real, though:**
- REC-4881 FAP data (43% polyp reduction) is genuinely promising and derisks the lead program.
- $2.1B market cap for a platform with 10+ clinical readouts coming is arguably cheap if you believe in the platform.
- If any program hits, the stock re-rates massively from $4.
- Bayer/Roche partnerships are validation from serious partners, not just hype.
- Trading 49% below estimated fair value (Simply Wall St).
- Merged entity has positioned for accelerated clinical timelines.

**Honest verdict on RXRX:** It could 2-3x from here or go to $1. The asymmetry is
real, but so is the binary risk. The consensus problem means it is unlikely to be the
stock that gives you the biggest edge -- the market has already "discovered" this
discovery company. If you buy it, size it small and accept the volatility.

## Risks

**Sector-wide risks:**
1. **No AI drug has been FDA approved yet.** The entire sector trades on a future that has not arrived. First approval expected 2026-2027, but delays are possible.
2. **Cash burn.** RXRX, ABSI, RLAY, and SDGR are all unprofitable. Dilution is the silent killer -- they will keep issuing shares to fund operations.
3. **Clinical failure.** AI improves odds but does not eliminate biology risk. One failed Phase 3 trial can crater any of these stocks 50-80% overnight. This is not a gradual decline -- it is a gap down.
4. **Regulatory uncertainty.** FDA draft guidance is promising but final rules (expected Q2 2026) could be more restrictive than hoped. The current FDA leadership environment adds unpredictability.
5. **Big Pharma competition.** Lilly, Novartis, and Pfizer are all building internal AI capabilities. If they succeed, the pure-play premium evaporates and these small companies become acquisition targets (good) or irrelevant (bad).
6. **Narrative exhaustion.** "AI" as a market buzzword may lose its premium as investors rotate to the next theme. We saw this with blockchain, metaverse, and clean energy.
7. **Interest rate environment.** Pre-revenue growth companies are rate-sensitive. If rates stay higher for longer, these stocks stay compressed.

**Timeline reality check:**
- 2026: First AI drug approval possible (60% probability). RLAY Phase 3, SDGR Phase 1 data, ABSI interim data. Major catalyst year.
- 2027-2028: If approvals materialize, pure plays with successful drugs re-rate significantly.
- 2029+: Revenue generation for companies with approved drugs begins.
- **You need a 3-5 year horizon minimum. This is not a 2026 swing trade.**
