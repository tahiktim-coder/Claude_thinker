# Sector 2: Energy Infrastructure for AI

## The Thesis
Every AI model needs massive electricity. Data centers consumed ~1.5% of global electricity in 2024, growing at 12% annualized. US data center demand is projected to climb from 19 GW (2023) to 35 GW (2030). Deloitte estimates AI data center power demand could grow by 3,000% by 2035. McKinsey projects $7 trillion in global AI data center infrastructure capex by 2030.

The US power grid was mostly built in the 1950s-1970s — ~70% is approaching end of life. This creates a massive bottleneck and investment opportunity.

**Key stat**: Data center deals hit a record $61 billion in 2025 (source: S&P Global / CNBC).

## The Consensus Check — WARNING

This trade is **already well-known**. "AI needs power" has been a major narrative throughout 2025. Nuclear stocks in particular have already had massive runs. Let's be honest about what's priced in.

## The Contrarian Risk: DeepSeek Effect
In January 2025, DeepSeek showed that AI can be run more efficiently than assumed. If AI models become dramatically more energy-efficient, the power demand thesis weakens. This is a real risk. However:
- Even with efficiency gains, TOTAL compute is growing faster than efficiency
- Jevons paradox: cheaper AI = more AI usage = more total power
- Grid modernization is needed regardless of AI

## ETF Options
- **URA** (Global X Uranium ETF) — pure uranium exposure
- **URNM** (Sprott Uranium Miners) — uranium miners
- **GRID** or utility-focused ETFs for broader infrastructure
- No great pure-play "AI power infrastructure" ETF exists yet

## Where the Edge Actually Is

The nuclear names (CEG, CCJ) have already run. The **less obvious plays** are:

1. **Grid infrastructure** (GEV, ETN, Quanta) — someone has to build and upgrade the actual wires, transformers, and switchgear. Less sexy, more edge.
2. **Cooling** (Vertiv) — AI chips run hot. Liquid cooling is a growing necessity.
3. **Regional utilities** with data center exposure (Entergy, Dominion) that haven't been repriced as "AI plays" yet.

## Risks
- Regulatory delays for nuclear (especially SMRs)
- AI efficiency improvements reducing demand growth
- Overbuilding risk — some data centers may never reach full capacity
- Interest rate sensitivity for capital-heavy utilities
- Political/policy changes

## Honest Verdict
The thesis is **rock solid** but the obvious plays (CEG, CCJ) are already crowded. The edge is in:
1. Grid infrastructure picks-and-shovels (GEV, ETN, Quanta) — everyone needs them
2. A small nuclear speculation (TLN or SMR, small position)
3. Avoid the most hyped names where the "AI power" narrative is fully baked in

**Conviction: 8/10** — strongest thesis of the three, but stock selection matters enormously.
